# AI Book — Personal Experience Angle (TBSoftWash)

## Positioning (what this book *is*)
A first-person, operator-written book about how adopting AI helped you:
- grow TBSoftWash
- protect premium positioning (top 50–75% pricing)
- stay relevant as search changes ("dying search engines" → LLM answers / AI search)

It’s not an AI textbook. It’s a **field guide + story + playbook**, written by someone running a real service business.

## Core promise
> If you run an exterior cleaning business, you can use AI to compete with bigger companies, respond faster, document better, and market smarter—without losing the craft.

## Key thesis (simple)
**AI doesn’t replace the work. It replaces the bottlenecks around the work.**
- speed to lead
- quoting clarity
- customer communication
- documentation/proof
- SOPs + training
- content creation and repurposing

## The “dying search engines” angle (how to frame it professionally)
Instead of saying Google is dead, frame it as:
- Discovery is fragmenting (Maps, socials, LLM answers, “zero-click” search)
- The winners will be brands with:
  - proof (photos/reviews)
  - clear process documentation
  - fast response systems
  - content that answers questions in the exact way buyers ask them

AI helps you produce that **consistently**, even as a small team.

## Reader
- Owner-operator or small team in exterior cleaning
- Wants to grow but is maxed out on time
- Doesn’t want gimmicks; wants systems

## Tone
Warm, plainspoken, operator-to-operator.
Professional structure. Minimal hype.
Use AI boldly (TTS/STT, automation), but keep claims provable and reviewed.

## Book structure (recommended)

### Part 1 — The moment you realize the game changed
1) “I run a real service business—here’s what started breaking”
   - slower ROI from traditional SEO
   - more competition, more noise
   - customers more educated, more skeptical
2) The new buyer journey (Maps + reviews + photos + AI answers)
3) What still matters (craft + trust) and what must change (systems)

### Part 2 — My AI adoption timeline (what I actually did)
4) Phase 0: stop chasing shiny tools (principles + guardrails)
5) Phase 1: AI for communication (texts/emails, quoting follow-ups)
6) Phase 2: AI for documentation (proof packs, checklists, SOPs)
7) Phase 3: AI for marketing (repurposing jobs into content)
8) Phase 4: AI for operations (training, QA, reducing callbacks)

### Part 3 — The AI workflows (templates you can copy)
9) Speed-to-lead system (scripts + timing)
10) Estimate assistant (scope intake → proposal draft)
11) Proof Pack system (before/after standards)
12) Review engine (ask + respond + reputation protection)
13) Content engine (job → post → local page → FAQ)

### Part 4 — Staying premium in an AI world
14) Why you don’t race to the bottom
15) How AI supports top-50–75% pricing (not discounting)
16) What to automate vs what must remain human
