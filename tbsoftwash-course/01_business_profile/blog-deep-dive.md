## Your “signature ideas” (what the blog shows about how you think)

### 1) Method-first: pressure is a tool, not the solution
Across posts you repeat the core philosophy: **chemistry + correct method** does the cleaning; pressure is situational.
- Soft wash described as garden-hose pressure where “chemicals do the work.”
- Roof: explicitly “do not pressure wash,” soft wash instead.

**Course use:** open the course with a decision tree:
- organic vs mineral vs physical contamination
- which surfaces tolerate pressure
- what is “damage” vs “clean”

### 2) Risk reduction is part of the product
You frame cleaning as protecting the asset (roof lifespan, water damage prevention via gutters/drains, slip hazards).
- Roof algae (Gloeocapsa) feeds on shingle filler → leads to moss/lichen → shingle lift/damage.
- Gutters/drains: water damage + mosquito breeding + flooding.

**Course use:** build a “why we cost more” lesson tied to *risk math* (replacement/repairs vs preventative care).

### 3) SOP mindset + repeatability
Several posts are basically SOPs in public:
- rust removal: pre-wet, apply, dwell, don’t dry, rinse, neutralize, disposal
- drain cleaning: inspection → prep → jetting sequence → test and report to customer
- roof measurement: Google Earth workflow + pitch factor + pricing guidance

**Course use:** turn these into field checklists + technician training cards.

### 4) Safety + chemical respect is non-negotiable
Your acid post is unusually explicit about PPE, ventilation, neutralization, and not mixing.
The formulas post includes warnings on:
- pre-wetting landscaping
- avoiding hot water in SH mixes
- not using calcium hypochlorite
- avoiding TSP (surface damage + potential environmental compliance issues)

**Course use:** make “chemical handling + incident response” a core module, not an appendix.

### 5) Practical, budget-aware engineering
You go deep on mechanical decisions:
- blend manifold: bigger fittings, reduce 90s, metering valve restriction, prefer gate valves
- belt-driven > direct-drive for heat/shock, buffer tank practicality
- hose repair in the field with reusable couplings

**Course use:** “operator engineering” lessons: build vs buy, common failure points, spare parts strategy.

### 6) Transparency about limitations / expectations
You explicitly set expectations:
- moss/lichen on roof: may take weeks to fall off as roots release
- gutter brightening: you can burn paint / expose bare metal; mitigation = touch-up paint
- rust removal: different acids, risks, and different efficacy

**Course use:** create canned customer scripts + “what can go wrong” sections that premium operators use to prevent disputes.

## Patterns to incorporate into the course (actionable)
- **Pre-wet / protect landscaping** appears repeatedly → codify as a branded standard.
- **Document everything** (before/after, pre-existing damage) → make mandatory for premium pricing defense.
- **Teach pricing as a function of complexity** (roof pitch factor, access, landscaping, height).
- **Upsell ladder** naturally emerges from your content: hard water, rust, oxidation, gutter brightening, paver sealing, drain jetting.
