# How To Brighten Gutters — Notes

## Source
- https://tbsoftwash.com/how-to-brighten-gutters/

## What the post is really about
Positioning **gutter brightening** as *restoration work* (separate from gutter cleaning and separate from a standard house wash), with clear risk framing and expectation setting.

## Key points
- Clarifies vocabulary:
  - **Gutter cleaning** = interior debris + downspout work.
  - **Gutter brightening** = exterior restoration using a dedicated product.
- Brightening is **not included** in standard house wash; it’s a distinct scope.
- Operational logic: if a customer only wants brightening, you still need a wash step first—so it usually makes sense to wash the whole house from the gutterline down.
- High risk callouts:
  - Strong product or aggressive brushing can remove paint / expose bare metal.
  - Extra caution around oxidized substrates (garage doors, etc.) to avoid “clean spots” that force an unintended extra scope.
- Customer expectation: sometimes the “shiny metal” outcome is pre-existing paint failure being revealed.
- Remedy: touch-up gutter paint (frame as a simple fix, not a panic).

## Signature phrases / quotable lines (short excerpts)
- “Gutter cleaning and gutter brightening … are obviously two completely different jobs…”
- “Gutter brightening does not come with a standard house wash.”

## Operator translation (how we teach this safely)
- Teach as **separate service** with a risk checklist:
  - oxidation check
  - paint integrity check
  - adjacent surface protection (garage doors/trim)
  - documentation before/after
- For public-facing course content: avoid giving “recipe ratios” as a step-by-step; focus on **process + risk discipline**.
