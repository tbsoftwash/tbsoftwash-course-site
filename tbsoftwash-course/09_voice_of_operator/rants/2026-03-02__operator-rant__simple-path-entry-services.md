## 1) Raw rant (verbatim, lightly cleaned for readability)

I wish I had someone show me this profession when I was younger.

This is an easy industry to start in.

Gutter cleaning would probably have been my focus. However, now, I would have told my younger self to go learn how to clean windows, and then start offering the service, along with any other exterior cleaning service possible.

I would have started out by renting pressure washers and surface cleaners if required, obviously factored into the cost. That would allow me to save up. Then I would have bought a cheap pressure washer to start, 4 gallons per minute, belt driven. That, coupled with some hose, a wand, a downstream injector, and a surface cleaner, and I am off to the races: house washing, driveways (what everybody thinks pressure washers do — the least of what I do/target when marketing), windows, gutters.

**Gutter cleaning:** easy entry: ladder, blower, contractor bags, rake, maybe a hose if necessary to snake downspouts. A gutter tool is immensely helpful, and as I have grown I have tools to vacuum gutters from the ground up to 2 stories, but starting out, a gutter tool and a ladder will suffice. Use the tool to push, pull, prod stuck leaves.

Charge accordingly (do not be the $99 guy). Too many kids/guys undercharge. You are risking your health. That is your most valuable asset in life. Without your health, you have nothing left. PPE is always paramount. Be careful.

**Window cleaning:** super easy entry: squeegee, mop, bucket, toolbelt, razor/scraper, aluminum poles (small/medium/large), Dawn, Bar Keepers Friend, and rags — lots of blue lint-free rags.

Cleaning windows is more technical than gutter cleaning. Gutters can be labor-intensive, and windows can be challenging to clean depending on layout and obstacles.

Again: charge accordingly. It’s technical and the people who want/are willing to pay for clean windows expect a good job. This is not a $15 car wash. This is a premium service. Treat it as such: clean professional, wear uniforms, utilize booties, have clean gloves handy.

A few jobs — real jobs quoted correctly — can begin to snowball.

That brings me to marketing.

You can have all the fancy tools, but without proper marketing, you’re likely going to fail.

When marketing, you can market to pressure washing or soft washing, yes — or you can step down and pick a specific service. I tried to focus on roof cleaning myself. That was a higher ticket item. Some pressure washers are unwilling to do it. There are loads of liability ramifications, but it’s a service that is needed and somebody has to do it.

If you get insured, including workers comp, you have likely positioned yourself into a very small group of pressure washing companies. This is when you begin to get on vendor lists with property management companies. Some will let you get on their vendor list with just general liability.

Having said that: when you start out, insurance is not a requirement. It’s smart, yes, and it can help land jobs, but there is no hard rule saying that you must be insured. You do have to check your local government for operating a pressure washing company — different story — but as far as insurance, not required, and you can begin making money before committing to a monthly bill.

Circling back: when I began marketing I marketed for that specific category, and wanted to distinguish myself as a company that soft washes first and pressure cleans as well — but we are targeting soft washing jobs, not multi-level parking garages. We have landed those, but those are being heavily targeted by the whales in this industry. If you’re just starting out, get some experience before trying to take on 20,000 sq ft of parking garage.

You may want to be a gutter cleaning company that offers exterior cleaning, or a window cleaning company that offers soft washing, etc.

We just felt a pressure washing company that specializes in soft wash played into a lot of exterior cleaning opportunities.

## 2) The core message (1-page summary)

- This business is approachable to start — but only if you keep it simple.
- Start with **entry services** that don’t require a $15k rig: **gutters and windows**.
- Use rentals early, then buy a **basic, reliable setup** (example: ~4 GPM belt drive + downstream + surface cleaner).
- **Charge like a pro**. Cheap pricing is not “marketing,” it’s a health risk and a business killer.
- **Premium services require premium behavior**: uniforms, booties, gloves, clean process.
- Tools help, but **marketing is the oxygen**.
- Specialization is real: pick a lane (roof, windows, gutters) or pick a strong umbrella brand — but don’t chase whale jobs early.
- Insurance helps you level up into vendor lists, but you can start earning first (local rules apply).

## 3) Beginner-friendly “translations” (what a novice should understand)

- **You don’t need the perfect rig to start.** You need a simple, safe setup + a plan to reinvest.
- **Gutters = low barrier, high risk.** Easy to learn, but ladders punish mistakes. Price and PPE like it matters.
- **Windows = low barrier, higher skill ceiling.** Easy to start, but harder to do well. Premium expectation.
- **The job isn’t just cleaning.** It’s: access, safety, customer experience, and proof.
- **Marketing is part of operations.** A perfect job nobody sees doesn’t scale.

## 4) Course-ready inserts (short blocks we can paste into lessons)

### Insert A — “The Simple Path” (short)
Most beginners overcomplicate this trade on Day 1. Don’t. Start with services that let you earn fast, learn fast, and reinvest: gutters and windows. Rent gear when you must, then buy basic reliable equipment. Quote like a pro, protect your health, and build momentum off real jobs done right.

### Insert B — “Do Not Be the $99 Guy” (short)
Undercharging isn’t hustle — it’s a liability plan. This work costs your body when you do it wrong, and ladders don’t care what the customer paid. Price so you can work safely, show up professionally, and still be here next year.

### Insert C — “Premium Service = Premium Behavior” (short)
Clean windows and clean gutters are premium services when done right. Dress like it. Protect the home (booties/gloves). Communicate clearly. Deliver a consistent finish. The customer isn’t paying for soap — they’re paying for certainty.

### Insert D — “Tools Don’t Replace Marketing” (short)
The newest toys don’t create calls. Marketing creates calls. Your job is to build a simple offer, show proof, and get in front of the right homeowners — then deliver.
