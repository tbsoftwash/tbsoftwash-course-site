# Roof Soft Wash SOP v2 (Operator)

## Service
Roof Soft Wash (asphalt shingle, tile, metal) — exterior-only cleaning focused on organic growth removal (algae/mildew/moss/lichen) using **low pressure** application and rinse.

> Operator principle (Tampa Bay Soft Wash standard): **Never pressure wash roofs.** Chemistry + dwell + gentle rinse. Protect landscaping. Document everything.

## Goal (definition of done)
- Visible organic growth is removed to the agreed standard **without surface damage**.
- Landscaping is protected and left healthy.
- Adjacent surfaces (windows, paint, screens, patios) are not left with residue/overspray.
- Proof Pack completed + delivered.
- Customer expectations are set for any slow-release improvements (when applicable).

## Safety / PPE
**Non‑negotiables**
- Sealed eye protection + chemical resistant gloves.
- Long sleeves/pants; consider apron.
- Slip resistant footwear.

**Fall risk policy**
- If it can be done safely from the ground, do it from the ground.
- If roof access is required: ladder safety + pitch/height limits per company policy.
- Stop work for lightning or wind that compromises spray control/footing.

**Electrical**
- Identify service drop, weatherheads, solar, exterior outlets, and cameras.

**Heat protocol**
- Follow: `04_sops/safety/heat-sun-hydration.md`

## Pre-checks (arrival)
### 1) Scope confirmation + roof type
- Confirm roof material: shingle / tile / metal.
- Identify problem areas: shaded/north planes, valleys, dormers, behind trees.
- Confirm scope boundaries: roof only vs roof + gutters + exterior walls.

### 2) Pre-condition documentation (CYA)
Capture pre-existing issues **before** any change:
- cracked/broken tiles
- missing/damaged shingles
- loose flashing
- prior repairs
- oxidized gutters (separate service: brightening)

### 3) Water access + runoff plan
- Confirm water source + hose routing.
- Identify runoff paths (downspouts, beds, ponds).
- Identify sensitive vegetation (hibiscus, palms, annuals).

### 4) Plant protection plan (mandatory)
- Pre-wet sensitive landscaping.
- Stage a dedicated rinse line.
- Assign responsibility: someone is always thinking about plants.

Reference: `02_chemicals/pages/landscaping-protection.md`

### 5) Customer expectation setting (2 minutes)
- “We don’t pressure wash roofs. This is a soft-wash process designed for safety.”
- “We will rinse and protect landscaping throughout.”
- “We’ll document before/after so you can see exactly what changed.”

## Chemicals (ranges + why) — operator-only
We do not publish exact recipes publicly.

Operator guidance:
- Strength is chosen based on roof material, growth severity/type, shade/moisture, and weather.
- Use adhesion/surfactant so product stays where you put it.
- Control overspray (wind awareness).

## Step-by-step workflow
### 1) Setup & protection
1. Start plant pre-wet.
2. Selective masking where needed.
3. Confirm wind direction + spray plan.
4. Confirm runoff plan.

### 2) Application (low pressure)
1. Apply evenly in controlled sections.
2. Hit heavy growth zones first (valleys/shaded planes).
3. Avoid forcing water under seams/edges.

### 3) Dwell + monitoring
1. Allow dwell based on field conditions.
2. Rinse plants during dwell cycles.
3. Watch for plant stress; respond immediately.

### 4) Rinse
1. Rinse appropriate to roof type and product used.
2. Maintain low pressure.
3. Final rinse of gutters/downspouts and nearby surfaces.

### 5) Touch-ups
- Re-hit stubborn spots only if safe; reassess risk before repeated passes.

### 6) Proof Pack
Follow: `04_sops/proof-pack/proof-pack-sop-v1.md`

## Quality checks (QA)
- Ground-view uniformity on all elevations.
- **Glass check:** no residue/spotting on windows.
- **Fixtures check:** no residue on lights, door hardware, cameras.
- No overspray/residue on windows/screens/paint.
- Landscaping looks healthy after final rinse.
- Driveways/walkways not left slick.

Reference:
- `02_chemicals/pages/glass-and-window-risk.md`
- `02_chemicals/pages/metals-fixtures-and-corrosion-risk.md`

## Common failure modes
- Plant stress/damage (insufficient pre-wet/rinse; concentrated runoff; wind drift)
- Overspray on adjacent surfaces
- Slow-release/ghosting expectations not set
- Tile fragility concerns (walking where you shouldn’t; pre-existing brittleness)
