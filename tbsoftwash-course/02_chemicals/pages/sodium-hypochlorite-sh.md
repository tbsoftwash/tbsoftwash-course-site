# Sodium Hypochlorite (SH)

## Chemical
Sodium hypochlorite — the primary oxidizer used for organic growth removal in exterior cleaning.

## Known as
- SH
- “Bleach” (customer term)
- Pool chlorine (not always the same strength)

## Used for
- Algae/mildew/organic growth removal on:
  - roofs (soft wash)
  - siding/soffits/trim (soft wash)
  - some concrete/flatwork organics (often as a pre/post treatment)

## Not used for / contraindications
- Not a universal “cleaner” for:
  - oxidation restoration
  - rust stains
  - hard-water mineral deposits
- Not appropriate where it creates unacceptable risk to:
  - delicate landscaping
  - fabrics/interiors
  - unprotected metals/finishes

## Typical dilution / range (operator)
We do not publish reckless recipes.

Operator guidance:
- Think in terms of **strength bands**, not one magic number.
- Start with the least aggressive band that reliably works for the soil and surface.
- Increase only when:
  - growth is heavier
  - surface tolerates it
  - you can control drift/runoff

Always test and document, and remember: **dwell + technique** beats “hotter mix”.

## Surfactant notes
- Surfactant/adhesion matters for roofs and vertical surfaces.
- Choose a surfactant that:
  - clings
  - rinses clean
  - helps you see coverage (if tinted)

## Dwell time
- Dwell is field-dependent (sun/heat/wind, growth severity, surface type).
- Watch the surface and the runoff; do not let product dry where it creates risk.

## PPE
- Sealed eye protection
- Chemical-resistant gloves
- Long sleeves/pants
- Respiratory protection if mist/wind conditions create inhalation risk

## Storage & transport
- Store upright, secured, and out of heat when possible.
- Label clearly.
- Never store where a leak can damage tools/vehicle.

## Neutralization / runoff
- Manage runoff like it matters (it does).
- Pre-wet and rinse landscaping continuously.
- Control downspout discharge when needed.

## Safe surfaces
- Used professionally on many exterior surfaces when applied correctly and rinsed.

## Avoid surfaces
- Interiors and anything not meant to be exposed to oxidizers.
- Any surface where drift/runoff cannot be controlled.

## Common failure modes
- Plant stress/damage from insufficient pre-wet/rinse
- Overspray on paint/windows/metals
- “It didn’t work” from under-dwell, uneven application, or oxidation misdiagnosis

## What to say to the customer (expectations)
- “We use a soft-wash process designed to remove organic growth safely. Pressure is not the cleaner.”
- “We protect landscaping and rinse throughout the job.”
- “Some staining/oxidation is not the same as mildew; we’ll set expectations before we start.”
